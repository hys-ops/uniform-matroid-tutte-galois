The Galois group of the Tutte polynomial of a uniform matroid

For every field K of characteristic zero and every 1 ≤ r < n, the ordinary Tutte polynomial of the uniform matroid Ur,n, viewed as a polynomial in x over K(y), has r distinct roots, and its Galois group realizes every permutation of them.

What this settles

This proves the uniform-matroid case of Bohn–Cameron–Müller’s Conjecture 9 for the ordinary two-variable Tutte polynomial. It is not a proof for all connected matroids. Their theorem for the multivariate invariant, with independent variables attached to the elements, is a different result.

In Theorem 4.7(1), Goodall, Jouve and Sereni already obtained the full group over ℚ(y) for fixed rank and sufficiently large ground sets. For prime corank greater than the rank, Theorem 4.7(2) gives primitivity and, for composite rank, double transitivity. Their cycle theorem (Theorem 4.5) also covers corank one in characteristic zero. The present argument covers all positive ranks and coranks uniformly, including algebraically closed coefficient fields. The variable y is an indeterminate. The singleton U1,1 lies outside Theorem 1.1 and has Tutte polynomial x and trivial group.

Proof map

1. Separate the two polynomial covers

Write s = n − r. In coordinates u = x − 1, v = y − 1, the curve is Fr(u) = C − Fs(v). The binomial derivative identity (3) makes the critical values determine their critical points; it also rules out 0 and −1. If the two covers shared a finite branch value, the critical points a and b would satisfy ab = 1. The hyperbola identity (4) at that pair then forces a = −1, a contradiction.

2. Preserve the symmetric group under base change

Simple critical points and distinct critical values give one transposition at each finite branch value of the first cover. Riemann–Hurwitz rules out a nontrivial connected cover of the line unramified over every finite point. Applied first to the finite inertia quotient and then to the intersection of the splitting fields, this gives the symmetric group and its preservation after the second cover.

3. Recover the original statement

Translating variables identifies the polynomial with the rank-sum Tutte polynomial. Passing from an algebraic closure of the coefficient field back to K preserves the full root-permutation action. Corollary 3.3 additionally counts s(r − 1) finite branch values of the projection to y; each has one double root and r − 2 simple roots for r ≥ 2.

Lean verification

The project formalizes Theorem 1.1 for the rank-sum polynomial of a bundled uniform matroid, including the root count and the natural action. The main declarations are UniformMatroidGalois.roots, UniformMatroidGalois.action, and UniformMatroidGalois.uniform_tutte_galois. Branch separation is UniformCritical.uniform_critical_values_disjoint. The exact branch-count formula in manuscript Corollary 3.3 is a written result, not one of the standalone audited Lean endpoints.

Lean 4.36.0. The bundled original log records 22 local modules, kernel replay and 12 public axiom audits. The audited endpoints use only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound. The proof first verifies that the normalization of the common fixed field is étale (AffineInertiaEtale.iSup_fixedField_etale), then uses NYLL’s theorem bijective_algebraMap_of_etale_polynomial_normalization. This is a pinned formal dependency, not a new axiom. The final geometric and arithmetic calculations are UniformGlobal.geometric and UniformGlobal.arithmetic.
